Virtual Piano - Guide

How to Play

Your computer keyboard works like the keys of a piano:

  • Low (left): Z X C V B N M
  • Middle (right): Q W E R T Y U
  • Black keys: S D ... 2 3 4

A MIDI keyboard connected to the computer (Menu, Settings) gets the same features: layer, pedal, arranger and track recording.

Instruments and Layers (Dual Voice)

  • Left / Right arrows : Change the Main Instrument
  • Up / Down arrows : Change Octave
  • Shift + Up / Down arrows : Change Note Velocity
  • F10 : Turn the Second Layer On/Off
  • Shift + Left / Right arrows : Change the Layer Instrument

Audio Controls

  • Space : Sustain Pedal (hold it to let notes ring)
  • Shift + Space : Pedal Lock (off, continuous, smart)
  • F1 / F2 : Transpose (change key)
  • F3 / F4 : Reverb
  • F5 : Metronome (set the BPM)
  • F6 / F7 : Chorus
  • F8 / F9 : Modulation

Favorites

Save your favorite sounds to switch quickly:

  • Save: Ctrl + 0-9
  • Load: Ctrl + Shift + 0-9

Arranger (Automatic Accompaniment)

While the arranger is on, the keys below the split point (C4 at first, which is the row from Z to M) stop sounding and choose the chord instead. Drums, bass and harmony play by themselves in the selected style, and the rest of the keyboard is free for the melody. The chord stays after you release the keys.

  • F11 : Start / Stop the arranger
  • Shift + F11 : Start with an intro, or finish with an ending if it is already playing
  • F12 : Fill-in
  • Shift + F12 : Switch variation A/B (with a fill-in)
  • Ctrl + Left / Right arrows : Change style (while playing, it changes on the next bar)
  • Ctrl + Up / Down arrows : Tempo in steps of 5 (with Shift, steps of 1)
  • Ctrl + PageUp / PageDown : Accompaniment volume
  • Ctrl + Shift + Left / Right arrows : Split point

Chords: a single key plays the major chord; Shift + the key plays the minor chord; root plus minor third plays the minor chord; root plus seventh plays the seventh chord. With three or more keys the program recognizes major, minor, seventh, ninth, diminished, augmented, sus and sixth chords, in any inversion. The ninth is the note a whole step above the root: C, E, G, B flat and D make C9, and C, D, E and G make Cadd9 (added ninth, no seventh). It also works for minor chords (Cm9) and with a major seventh (Cmaj9); in ninth chords with a seventh the fifth may be left out.

Record a Style by Playing

In the Arranger menu choose Record a New Style by Playing (starts from nothing) or Re-record Parts of the Current Style (starts from an existing style). You record one part at a time, always playing in C major; afterwards the arranger moves everything to whatever chord you play.

The parts are: Drums, Bass, Chords and Pad of variation A, the same four of variation B, and the Fill-in. Whatever is left empty in variation B and in the fill-in uses variation A.

  • Ctrl + T : Next part (with Shift, the previous one)
  • Ctrl + R : Record. There is a one-bar count-in; play, then press it again to stop. It records up to 4 bars; the fill-in, only 1.
  • Ctrl + Q : Quantize: changes the figure your playing is snapped to: quarter notes, eighth notes, sixteenth notes, eighth-note triplets (swing), sixteenth-note triplets or thirty-second notes (with Shift, the previous one). It applies to the next recordings, so you can record the kick in sixteenth notes and the hi-hat in triplets.
  • Ctrl + Left / Right arrows : How far up the chords the part goes. The selected bass, chords or pad goes up from where it was played in C as far as this chord, and goes down on the chords after it. At first it is F sharp (F#): up as far as F#, down from G on. It applies to both variations.
  • Ctrl + N : Seventh and ninth: turns on or off, for the selected chords or pad, the seventh and the ninth the arranger adds by itself. It starts on. It applies to both variations.
  • Ctrl + P or F11 : Listen to the style as it is
  • Ctrl + Shift + P : Listen to the selected part only (just the bass, for instance)
  • Ctrl + L : Listen to the last recording only: just what you have just recorded (the snare, for instance), without what was already there
  • Ctrl + Z : Undo the last recording. Got the snare wrong? Only that take goes away; the hi-hat and cymbal recorded before it stay. Press it again to go back one more recording.
  • Ctrl + Delete : Erase the selected part, with all its instruments
  • Ctrl + Up / Down arrows : Tempo of the style
  • Ctrl + S : Save the style and leave
  • Esc : Leave without saving

While the recorder is open, the Style Recorder menu has the same commands, plus Erase Everything and Start Over. Under Quantize Recordings to you pick the figure straight from the list.

  • Drums and Fill-in: the keys become the drum kit: Z kick, S rim, X snare, D clap, G closed hi-hat, H pedal hi-hat, J open hi-hat, V B N M , . toms, L crash, ; ride. Every recording adds pieces to what is already there, so you can record kick and snare first and the hi-hat afterwards.
  • Fill-in: it is one bar long, so its recording stops by itself at the end of the bar. While you record the fill-in, only the fill-in plays: the metronome and the pieces already in it, without the bass, the chords, the pad and the drums of variation A.
  • Bass, Chords and Pad: what you play is recorded as you played it: the notes, their octave, how long each one lasts and every repeated hit. Recording only changes the timing, by snapping the notes to the quantize figure in use.
  • Several instruments in the same part: on bass, chords and pad every recording adds too, and the instrument is the one selected when you record. If the part does not have that instrument yet, it comes in beside the ones already there: the Chords can be an electric piano, a guitar and an organ. If the part already has that instrument, the new notes join its notes. A style holds up to 6 instruments in all, not counting the drums. As you go through the parts with Ctrl + T, the program says the instruments of each one. To redo something, undo with Ctrl + Z, or erase the part with Ctrl + Delete and record it again.
  • In variation B, an instrument with nothing recorded there yet starts from nothing: while you record, what it plays in variation A is silent, and the other instruments play as usual.
  • Afterwards the arranger moves those notes to the chord you play: the third, the fifth and the seventh follow the type of the chord (E becomes E flat on a minor chord) and the other notes just move along with the root. The part goes up to the new root (as far as F sharp) or down to it (from G on), to stay close to where it was played; that limit is changed with Ctrl + Left / Right arrows. Inside the recorder everything is heard in C, so the difference only shows after saving, on other chords.
  • Seventh and ninth: in the chords and the pad, when you play a chord with a seventh and the recording has no seventh, the highest C of the part plays the seventh instead: on C7, E, G and C become E, G and B flat. On a ninth chord the ninth comes in just above it, in the same rhythm: on C9, E, G, B flat and D. The bass stays on the root, and what was recorded with a seventh or a ninth is left as it is. To have the part play only what was recorded, turn this off with Ctrl + N.
  • What you play is snapped to the quantize figure in use (at first, the one of the style).

Edit Styles as Text

Every style is a text file, including the ones you record by playing. In the Arranger menu choose Edit a Copy of the Current Style as Text: the program copies the selected style to the folder Documents, Piano Virtual, Styles and opens the copy in Notepad, with the instructions at the top of the file. Edit it, save, then choose Reload Styles.

Every pattern has one character per step. With 4 steps per beat, a 4/4 bar has 16 characters:

[drums]
kick  = x... .... x.x. ....
snare = .... x... .... x...
hat   = x.x. x.x. x.x. x.x.

[bass]
program = 33
a = 1--- --1. ..1- 5-3.
  • Drums: . silence, x hit, X accent, o soft hit
  • Bass: 1 root, 3 third, 5 fifth, 7 seventh or sixth, 8 octave
  • Chords: x whole chord, 1 to 4 single notes (arpeggio)
  • - keeps the previous note sounding

What you record by playing is saved in another way, with one line for each note, as it sounds over a C major chord (middle C is C4). You can write it like that too:

[bass]
program = 33
a C2 = x--x .... .... x---
a G1 = .... x-.x .... ....
a C3 = .... .... x--. ....

In these lines, x is the note, X an accented note, o a soft note and - keeps the note sounding. For variation B, start the line with b instead of a. The line up = F# says how far up the chords those notes go; it can be any note from C to B. In [chord] and [pad], the line tensions = off makes the part play only what is written, without the seventh and the ninth the arranger adds. A part with more than one instrument has a section for each: [chord], [chord 2], [chord 3]. A style can have up to six instrument sections.

Song (Track Recording)

Record up to 6 tracks, one at a time, each with its own instrument. While you record a track, the others play. If the arranger is on during the recording, the accompaniment is recorded too, on a track of its own.

  • Ctrl + R : Record / stop recording on the selected track (there is a one-bar count-in)
  • Ctrl + P : Play / Stop
  • Ctrl + T : Next track (with Shift, the previous one)
  • Ctrl + M : Mute / unmute the selected track
  • Ctrl + Q : Quantize the selected track: snaps the notes to the time grid (eighth, sixteenth, triplet). The strength sets how far: 100% lands on the grid, 50% only moves half way. Song, Undo Quantize brings back what you played.
  • Ctrl + N / O / S : New song / Open / Save as a MIDI file

Recording again on a track replaces what was on it. To record the accompaniment again, select the accompaniment track and use Song, Clear Track.

Recording tip: The metronome is heard on your sound device, but it does not go into the recorded audio file.
